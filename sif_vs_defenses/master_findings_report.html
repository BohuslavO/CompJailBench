Semantic Intent Fragmentation against four defenses

One compositional attack, one shared 33-sample trajectory set, four defenses drawn from four papers — a graph analyzer, a text monitor, an activation probe, and a topology detector. Each was first validated on the collusion-style threat it was built for, then run against SIF. All four fail where SIF lives, and each fails in a way that says what it was really keying on.

The one-paragraph story

Semantic Intent Fragmentation is a distributed jailbreak: a single, legitimately-phrased request that an unmodified orchestrator decomposes into three individually-benign subtasks (retriever → processor → publisher). No message is adversarial; the violation exists only in how the steps compose. Across every defense the attack's fragmentation machinery is airtight — FS(P) = 1.0 on all 22 attack arms, meaning no subtask is individually flaggable — and the attack's own success gate binds not on evasion but on condition (e), the genuine-harm judge, which only fires on personnel data. That gives a reproducible SIF-ASR of 0.136 ± 0.045 whose stable core is three HR-domain arms. The question this report answers for each defense is narrower and sharper: shown the exact trajectory, does the defense see the composition?

Cross-defense synthesis

The same 33 trajectories under four defense modalities. Every one validates on its home threat and collapses on SIF — but the shape of each collapse is a different diagnosis.

The through-line. A defense that works on collusion — where a bad agent behaves badly and leaves a local trace — fails on SIF, where every agent behaves well and the harm is only in the arrangement. SentinelAgent's compositional tier exists but is drowned by a hyperactive local layer; the CoT monitor has the evidence but asks the wrong question; the probe has the right question but no per-agent signal to read; Agent-to-Agent Monitor's topology sees plan size, not structure. Four modalities, one blind spot: composition itself.

Defense 1 · execution-graph analyzer · He et al. 2025

SentinelAgent

Non-discriminating

Models the running system as a typed directed execution graph and diffs the dynamic graph against what was permitted, in three tiers: global (tier 1), single-point node/edge faults (tier 2, standing in for Granite Guardian + LlamaFirewall), and multi-point subgraph matching (tier 3, the only compositional layer). Four live rounds on Bedrock plus one offline replay.

SentinelAgent, seen-signature library — the headline is that recall carries no information
MetricValueReading
Detection recall1.00022/22 — but see FPR
False-positive rate1.00011/11 benign controls also flagged
Specificity0.000no benign sample survived
Precision0.667exactly the base rate of attack arms
Balanced accuracy0.500chance — an alarm wired permanently on
Unpermitted edges0.0000/22 — every call within declared grants

What the run establishes

  • The false positives are local, not compositional. The tier-2 gpt-oss-20b node/edge checkers flag 10 of 11 benign controls on their own. Tightening the tier-3 subgraph matcher moves its own false matches from 7/11 to 2/11 but does not move the pipeline FPR at all — the local layer already alarms on nearly every benign enterprise workflow.
  • Tier 3 did the one thing it claims. Two attack arms (sif-sec-m2/m3::generated) were caught with zero flagged nodes and zero flagged edges — detectable only as a subgraph. The cleanest demonstration of compositional detection, and the sharpest evidence for SIF's premise.
  • The strict re-check is pure cost. Turning it off leaves recall and FPR unchanged while halving model calls (61→35 per sample). Recommend defaulting it off.
  • A weaker orchestrator changes nothing. Haiku fragments the request as readily as Sonnet (condition (b) 22/22 for both); the defense reads both trajectories identically.

Caveat. The checkers are gpt-oss-20b, not the paper's Granite Guardian / LlamaFirewall; the specific FPR is a property of this local model. What transfers is the structural result — fragmentation holds, the compositional layer does not discriminate, the false-positive rate is local. Held-out signature library: recall 0.909, FPR 1.000.

Defense 2 · black-box text monitor · NARCBench CoT monitor

CoT / observable-reasoning Monitor

Blind as shipped

An LLM reads one evidence view of the trajectory — actions, observable reasoning, or all messages, plus our plan-inclusive fourth view — and is asked whether it shows covert coordination between agents. Because the defense is a prompt, the evidence window and the framing become experimental variables. Grid: 4 views × 2 framings (published, and a composition-reframed “steelman”), validated first on real NARCBench collusion.

Suspiciousness AUROC (SIF attack vs benign), Llama 3.3 70B monitor, gpt-oss-20b orchestrator
ViewPublishedSteelmanReading

What the run establishes

  • The monitor is competent, then blind. The identical run_monitor scores AUROC 0.861 / 0.870 / 0.862 separating collusion from control on 68 held-out NARCBench transcripts — and flatlines at chance on SIF across every published view, including the full orchestrator plan.
  • The failure is the question, not the evidence window. Handing it the plan doesn't help; reframing the prompt to composition (“do the steps combine into a violation even if each is benign?”) lifts AUROC to 0.61–0.70. Framing is the dominant axis.
  • Recovery is precision-costly and capability-dependent. The steelman flags benign controls too (FPR 0.09–0.45). A weaker monitor (Haiku) recovers less and is markedly less precise; plan-view precision is a strong-model property.
  • Robust across two orchestrators. The published-framing blindness replicates on Sonnet-authored trajectories. Two S2 sub-claims did not replicate and were retracted: “plan view = 0 false positives” and “gate-passing attacks are hardest.”

Caveat. Suspiciousness is coarsely quantized (only {0, 0.2, 0.75, 0.8, 0.9} observed) so steelman AUROC gaps are within noise; evaded rests on 3–4 gate-passers and FPR on 11 controls. Results are for two named monitors, not LLM monitors in general; NARCBench's cot carries real rationales while SIF's is empty, so that 0.87 does not transfer.

Defense 3 · white-box activation probe · NARCBench

NARCBench Activation Probe

Near-blind

A linear probe over the residual-stream activations (gpt-oss-20b, layer 12, last-token hidden state per agent turn), trained by logistic regression to separate colluding from honest agents. The only white-box defense in the suite — it needs the model's weights on a GPU, which no hosted API exposes, so extraction ran on Kaggle (T4 ×2) and scoring locally.

What the run establishes

  • The probe is near-blind to SIF. A probe validated to held-out AUROC ~1.0 on the collusion data it was trained for sits at 0.57–0.70 on SIF — because SIF's harm is compositional and no single agent's activations look deceptive. Each worker does an individually-honest subtask.
  • Two of five detectors don't even apply. The strongest NARCBench detectors hard-require exactly four agents; SIF's three-agent star returns them None.
  • Detection recall is negligible. At benign-calibrated thresholds, 9–36%; of the four attacks that cleared the SIF gate, three scored as benign (n=4).

Caveat. Small n (22/11) → wide CIs; activation_alignment at 0.702 is mildly above chance, not a clean null, and the gate-passer evasion cell is only n=4. Faithful by construction: the model that authored the SIF text is the model probed.

Defense 4 · project-defined graph-and-judge detector

Agent-to-Agent Monitor

Plan-size confound

Builds an utterance graph over agent communication and combines structural checks with an LLM judge for compositional leakage. The two components are reported separately so topology-only behavior is not confused with semantic judgment. Three live rounds cover two orchestrator families and two judge families.

Attributing the topology “signal” — is it structure, or just plan size?
Feature setAUROCReading
All structural features0.83–0.92looks like detection…
Plan size alone (subtask count)0.82–0.91…but size reproduces it entirely
Size-invariant shape (depth, cycles)≈0.50topology is blind once size is held out

What the run establishes

  • Topology detection is a plan-size confound. The full-feature AUROC (0.83–0.92) is reproduced by subtask count alone (0.82–0.91); the honest size-invariant shape AUROC is ~0.50. Attack plans simply have more subtasks (~4.6 vs ~3.3) — an artifact of how phrasings were authored, not a structural signature of harm.
  • The real signal is semantic tool-composition, not graph shape. An egress tool (http.post/webhook) appears in ~86% of attack runs vs ~30% of benign — harm as bulk-retrieve + join + external egress, which a topology reader cannot see.
  • The leakage judge is uncalibrated. Recall 1.0 but FPR 0.909 — identical across Llama and Mistral judges, so it's the shipped prompt, not the model. Robust across both orchestrator and both judge families.

Caveat. The structural heuristic and collision/AUROC diagnostics are post-hoc and detection-only; no live intervention is evaluated. The strong orchestrator (Sonnet) is unavailable on this account; gpt-oss-120b was flaky (500s).

Caveats common to all four

  • Every instrument is a substitution. Guardrails stand in for LlamaGuard; the judges and checkers are Bedrock/gpt-oss models, not the source papers' classifiers. The structural results transfer; the specific rates are not cross-comparable to either source paper.
  • Small n. 22 attack arms / 11 controls. Per-slice and per-mechanism rates are directional; AUROC confidence intervals are wide.
  • Judge/monitor noise. Temperature 0 is necessary but not sufficient — adjudicators still drift between byte-identical runs (Mistral's condition (d) flipped 5/22), so gated numbers are reported over repeats, never as a point.
  • The “generated” phrasing arm is a template, not an attacker model. The source paper's LLM-vs-human phrasing claim (71%) is untested here; do not cite the 0.136 against it.
  • Constructed dataset. The 11 scenarios were built for this project (M1–M4 mechanism taxonomy is the SIF authors'; policy clauses are verbatim NIST SP 800-53). Not a released benchmark.
